
# Chapter 1: Introduction

## 1.1 Statement of the Problem

Many electronics retail outlets manage their operations through a collection of disconnected tools and manual procedures. Activities such as point of sale transactions, contract administration, payment recording, and employee duty tracking are often handled in separate systems or on paper. This fragmentation creates inefficiencies, increases the risk of human error, and makes oversight and reporting more difficult. The absence of a single, integrated platform impedes the timely sharing of information among staff and managers and complicates the customer experience when transactions and records are inconsistent.

## 1.2 Objectives of the Work

This project aims to design and develop an integrated software system that unifies customer facing commerce with the internal management needs of an electronics store. The principal objectives are to:

- Provide a unified platform that combines online product browsing and purchasing with the store’s internal operational tools.
- Support point of sale processes so that in person sales are recorded alongside online transactions in the same system.
- Centralize contract handling and payment records to reduce duplication and improve financial tracking.
- Enable basic employee management, including the recording of duty hours and assignment of tasks related to store operations.
- Implement role based access so that customers, store workers, and administrators have appropriate privileges and interfaces.

Collectively, these objectives seek to reduce administrative burden, minimize errors arising from manual coordination, and provide clearer operational visibility for store management.

## 1.3 Scope of the Work

The scope of this project is deliberately focused on core retail operations appropriate for a medium sized electronics store. The system will include web and mobile client interfaces to serve the staff and will be suitable for local or on premises deployment. The work concentrates on essential features: product catalog and sales, point of sale recording, contract and payment management, employee duty-hour tracking, and basic project/task coordination for internal use. The project does not extend to large scale enterprise integrations, extensive third party service deployments, or features beyond these core operational needs.

## 1.4 Significance of the Work

Developing a unified system for store operations offers practical and academic value. Practically, it can increase operational efficiency by consolidating records and reducing time spent on reconciliation and manual data entry. Centralized record keeping improves accuracy in financial and human resource tracking and supports better decision making by management. For customers, a more coherent system can lead to a smoother purchasing experience and more consistent service. From an academic perspective, the project demonstrates the application of software engineering principles to a realistic domain problem, illustrating design trade offs in integrating multiple functional concerns within a single system.
